Moemon Music Studio 1.5 "8-Bit Encore!": any song as NES or Game Boy music, NES music in, and a GREAT BALL icon.

## CHIPTUNE
- **Chiptune...** (top bar) arranges the open song as NES or Game Boy music, whatever it came from: a MIDI, a project,
  or any rip the studio plays (PlayStation 1 and 2, N64, Game Boy, NES, DS, GBA, Super Nintendo, Genesis).
- **Arranged like a chip musician would.** The studio works out what each part is: the melody, its harmony, the bass,
  the drums, ostinatos, pads and figures, and which parts are only echoes or doublings of another. The melody plays on
  square 1, its harmony on square 2, the bass on the triangle (NES) or wave channel (Game Boy), the drums on the noise.
  It reads how the notes behave, not which instrument plays them, so it works for any song and any instrument.
- **Real chip instruments.** The melody on a 25% square near full volume, the harmony on a contrasting width under it,
  chords under the melody as quick 12.5% arpeggios, the melody's echo on square 2 later and softer, a little bite on
  each note's attack and a delayed vibrato on long melody notes. Each instrument keeps its own shape, and the song's
  swells are kept but squeezed the way a chip mix is.
- **A drum kit.** Every hit becomes a kick, snare, hi-hat, open hi-hat or crash, played the way chip drums are; on the
  NES kicks and snares are DMC samples, so the hi-hats keep the noise channel.
- **Steady octaves.** Each line keeps to one register, the way it would be written for one channel: square 2 and the
  bass stay where their line is, chords arpeggiate within an octave, the melody keeps to its upper voice, and a quick
  flourish that jumps an octave away plays nearer. Harmonies kept, sweeps and slides followed exactly.
- **The arrangement table.** Every part of the song, what the studio reads it as, and what it plays as: change any part
  to Melody, Harmony, Backing, Echo, Bass or Off, and set its duty and octave.
- **Real NES and Game Boy files.** It saves an `.nsf` or a `.gbs` with its own little sound driver, so it plays in any
  NSF / GBS player (and on a flash cart), keeps its loop, and opens in the studio again like any other NES or Game Boy
  song: hear it on the console's own chip (Original), export it for Moemon, or make it chiptune for the other console.

## NES music
- **Open NSF and NSFe files** (and NEZplug .m3u playlists), every song in them one by one, in the playlist too.
- The NES's squares, triangle and noise play on the GBA's own Game Boy channels; DMC samples come in as samples.
  **Convert NES > GBA** makes them GBA instruments, as for Game Boy songs.
- The cartridges' extra sound chips are read too: Konami's VRC6 and VRC7, Namco 163, Sunsoft 5B, the Famicom Disk System
  and MMC5.

## And
- The studio has an icon: a GREAT BALL, on the window and on the Windows exe.

## Download
Windows: unzip **MoemonMusicStudio-windows.zip** and run MoemonMusicStudio.exe.
Linux: unpack **MoemonMusicStudio-linux.tar.gz** and run moemon_music_studio (needs SDL2: `sudo apt install libsdl2-2.0-0`).
